Overthinking can feel productive because your mind is busy. You replay possibilities, search for certainty, and try to prevent every negative outcome.

The problem is that thinking can quietly replace deciding.

## Name what is happening

Start by saying the truth plainly: **I am looking for certainty that this decision cannot provide.**

Naming the pattern creates distance between you and the thought. You are not the overthinking loop. You are the person noticing it.

## Regulate before you solve

Take one slow breath and make the exhale longer than the inhale. Relax your shoulders. Place both feet on the floor. Give your nervous system evidence that this moment is manageable.

You make better decisions when your body is not treating uncertainty like an emergency.

## Reduce the decision

Ask yourself three questions:

1. What do I know right now?
2. What is actually within my control?
3. What is the smallest useful action I can take next?

You do not need the entire path. You need a next step that creates new information.

## Set a decision deadline

Give yourself a reasonable amount of time, then decide. A deadline prevents research and reflection from becoming endless avoidance.

Clarity often appears after movement—not before it.

Overthinking loses power when you stop asking it for permission to act.
